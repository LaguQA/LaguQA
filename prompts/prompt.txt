SECTION 1 — THE FIVE MISTAKES THAT ACTUALLY HAPPENED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

These are not hypothetical. They were measured on real output from this exact
task. Four of them are duration or register errors and together they caused
every failed bar. Read this section twice.

M1. A DIGIT WITH NO BEAM IS A FULL BEAT, NOT HALF A BEAT.
    This single mistake produced nine failed bars in one song, all identical.
    A bare digit with nothing drawn above it lasts one full beat. At L:1/8 that
    is TWO units, written "A2". It is not "A".

M2. THE VALUE OF A DOT DEPENDS ON THE BEAM ABOVE THE DOT ITSELF.
    A dot extends the note before it. How much it extends by is decided by
    whether that dot sits under a beam:
        dot with NO beam above it        -> adds 2 units (a full beat)
        dot UNDER a single beam          -> adds 1 unit (half a beat)
    Do not give every dot a fixed value. Look above each dot.

M3. DO NOT INVENT SIXTEENTH NOTES.
    "/2" means a sixteenth and requires TWO stacked beam lines above the digit.
    One beam line is an eighth, written as a plain letter with no suffix.
    Writing "c/2B/2" for singly beamed digits is wrong.

M4. AN UNDOTTED DIGIT IS NOT IN THE UPPER OCTAVE.
    Octave errors survive beat checking, because the durations stay correct.
    They are caught only by range. See Section 3 for the mapping and Section 6
    for the range test you must run before answering.

M5. A SLUR MEANS ONE SYLLABLE OVER TWO NOTES.
    When a curved line joins two digits under a single printed syllable, the
    second note takes "_" in the w: line, not a repeat of the syllable.

M6. NEVER WRITE A DOT OR A DASH INTO THE ABC.
    The dot and the dash are jianpu glyphs. They have no meaning as durations
    in ABC. In ABC a bare "." is a staccato mark, so writing "c2 . d c B"
    produces a staccato and loses the beat entirely.
    A dot is consumed by ADDING its value to the number on the note before it.

        printed  "3 . 3 3 2"  with a beam over the middle "3 3"
        wrong    F4 . F F E2         the dot leaked through
        right    F4 F F E2           the dot is already inside the "4"

    After you finish a bar, scan it: if any "." or "-" survives as a standalone
    token, you have not finished converting it.

M7. DO NOT COPY THE SHAPE OF THE EXAMPLES.
    The two bars in Section 2 have opposite layouts on purpose. One starts with
    an unbeamed note and ends with beamed ones; the other does the reverse.
    Beams fall wherever the engraver put them. Read each bar's beams from the
    image every time. Never assume a bar begins or ends a particular way.

SECTION 2 — WORKED EXAMPLE (memorise the shape of this bar)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Printed on the page, in a song headed "Do = d" and "4/4":

        1'  7  1'  .  7   6  5
            ‾‾‾‾‾  ‾‾‾‾   ‾‾‾‾
        (the overlines show which digits share a beam; the first 1' has none,
         and the dot shares a beam with the 7 that follows it)

Read it glyph by glyph:

    1'   no beam                    -> d2      2 units
    7    one beam                   -> c       1 unit
    1'   one beam, then a dot that
         is itself under a beam     -> d2      1 unit + 1 unit from the dot
    7    one beam                   -> c       1 unit
    6 5  one beam, curved line      -> (B A)   1 unit + 1 unit
                                       -----------------------
                                       total   8 units = 4/4  CORRECT

    correct output:  d2 c d2 c (B A)
    wrong output:    d c d3 c/2B/2 A      only 7 units, rejected

EXAMPLE B — the opposite layout. Printed in a song headed "Do = C" and "4/4":

        6  6  7  6  5
           ‾‾‾‾‾
        ‿‿‿‿‿
        (only the SECOND and THIRD digits share a beam. The first digit has no
         beam. The last two digits have no beam. The curved line underneath
         joins digits one and two, and one printed syllable sits under them.)

Read it glyph by glyph:

    6    no beam                    -> A2      2 units
    6    one beam                   -> A       1 unit
    7    one beam                   -> B       1 unit
    6    no beam                    -> A2      2 units
    5    no beam                    -> ^G2     2 units
                                       -----------------------
                                       total   8 units = 4/4  CORRECT

    correct output:  (A2 A) B A2 ^G2
    wrong output:    A2 A B A ^G           only 6 units, rejected

    The slur wraps digits one and two, so the lyric is "Bu- _ ngong jeum- pa":
    one syllable plus one "_" for the slurred pair, then one syllable per note.

COMPARE THE TWO EXAMPLES. In example A the bar ENDS with beamed eighth notes.
In example B it ends with unbeamed full beats. Both are 8 units. The only way
to tell them apart is to look at where the beams are drawn. There is no default
shape, and the ending of one bar tells you nothing about the next.

SECTION 3 — PITCH: BUILD THE TABLE BEFORE YOU WRITE ANY NOTE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.1 ABC OCTAVE LETTERS. Get this right or everything shifts.

        C,  D,  E,  F,  G,  A,  B,     octave below middle C
        C   D   E   F   G   A   B      C here IS MIDDLE C
        c   d   e   f   g   a   b      the octave above middle C
        c'  d'  e'  f'  g'  a'  b'     two octaves above middle C

    Uppercase C is middle C. Lowercase c is one octave HIGHER than uppercase C.
    A comma lowers one octave, an apostrophe raises one octave.

3.2 CHOOSING THE REGISTER. Read the printed "Do = X" or "1 = X". Then place the
scale so that most notes of the song land between C (middle C) and c. Songs in
this book are written for ordinary voices and almost never leave that area.

    Example, Do = C:
        1=C  2=D  3=E  4=F  5=G  6=A  7=B   1 with dot above = c
        5 with dot below = G,

    Example, Do = D, key signature K:D:
        1=D  2=E  3=F  4=G  5=A  6=B  7=c   1 with dot above = d
        F and c are automatically sharp because of K:D, do not write ^

    Example, Do = G, key signature K:G:
        If the melody dwells on 1' and 2' you have placed the scale too high.
        Try 1=G, and check again. Pick the placement that keeps the song inside
        the singable area.

3.3 OCTAVE DOTS. A dot centred directly ABOVE a digit raises it one octave. A
dot directly BELOW lowers it one octave. Two dots means two octaves. These dots
sit on the digit's vertical axis. Do not confuse them with the duration dot,
which is printed to the RIGHT of a digit at mid height, nor with print specks
and show-through from the reverse page.

3.4 KEY SIGNATURE. K: comes strictly from the printed "Do = X". Do not change
it because the melody sounds minor. Many songs here are printed "Do = C" while
the tune centres on 6 and ends on 6. That is the relative minor, and K:C is
still the correct key signature. Note it with a comment and move on:

    % melodi berpusat pada 6 dan berakhir di 6, minor relatif

3.5 ACCIDENTALS. A printed sharp or flat before a digit maps to "^" or "_"
before the note letter. A digit with a diagonal stroke through it is this
book's way of writing a raised note; read it as a sharp, write "^", and add a
comment recording it.

SECTION 4 — DURATION ENGINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1 Always use L:1/8. One beat is 2 units. A 4/4 bar holds 8 units, 3/4 holds 6,
2/4 holds 4, 6/8 holds 6.

4.2 Duration from the beam count above a digit:

        no beam           one full beat        2 units      write "A2"
        one beam          half a beat          1 unit       write "A"
        two beams         quarter of a beat    1/2 unit     write "A/2"

4.3 Duration dots, printed to the right of a digit:

        dot with no beam above it      adds 2 units
        dot under one beam             adds 1 unit
        several dots in a row          add their values in sequence

    So "5 . . 0" with nothing beamed is A held three beats then a one beat rest,
    written "A6 z2". And "5 ." with no beams is A held two beats, "A4".

4.4 The digit 0 is a rest. It takes its duration from the same rules and is
written "z" or "z2". A rest never carries a lyric syllable.

4.5 Dashes after a digit each add one full beat, same as an unbeamed dot.

4.6 Triplets are printed as a bracket marked 3 over three digits. Write them as
"(3" followed by the three notes. Verify the group total on the grid.

4.7 Anacrusis. If the first bar is short, transcribe it short. The first and
last bar together should add up to one full bar. Do not pad either of them.

SECTION 5 — LYRICS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

5.1 Put each verse on its own w: line, placed directly after the music line it
belongs to. Two verses under one system means two consecutive w: lines.

5.2 Token rules:

        -    separates syllables inside one word:      In- jit
        _    holds the previous syllable over one more note (slur, melisma)
        *    skips a note that carries no syllable

5.3 The count must balance exactly. Number of tokens on a w: line must equal the
number of PITCHED notes on its music line. Rests are not counted and take no
token. Count both sides before you move on.

5.4 Copy the hyphenation from the page, even where the book splits a word oddly.
Do not add, merge, or drop syllables to make the arithmetic work. If the count
does not balance, you have misread a note or a slur, so go back and re-read the
image rather than editing the lyrics.

SECTION 6 — MANDATORY SELF-CHECK BEFORE YOU ANSWER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Run all five. Do not show your working; just fix what fails, then output.

C1. BAR SUMS. For every bar, add the units and compare with the meter quota.
    Every interior bar must match exactly. Fix any that do not.

C2. RANGE. Find your lowest and highest note and count the semitones between
    them. Songs in this book span roughly 5 to 14 semitones. If yours exceeds
    17, you have almost certainly put a group of notes in the wrong octave.
    Go back to Section 3.1 and re-place the scale.

C3. LEAPS. No two consecutive notes should be more than an octave apart. If any
    pair is, one of the two is in the wrong octave.

C4. SYLLABLES. For each w: line, token count equals pitched-note count.

C5. SHAPE. First line is %abc-2.1. All fields from Section 0.3 are present, in
    that order, with "-" where empty. The reply is one fenced abc block and
    contains nothing else.

SECTION 7 — THINGS NOT TO DO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

7.1 Do not complete a song from memory. Transcribe only what the image shows.
    If you recognise the tune, use that only to break a tie between two readings
    that the evidence leaves equally likely, and say so in a comment.

7.2 Do not add slurs, ties, ornaments, dynamics, repeats, or chords that are not
    printed.

7.3 Do not silently correct the book. If the printed text has an obvious typo,
    reproduce it and note it:

        % buku menulis "Simanjunatak"

7.4 Do not force a bar to balance by changing a pitch. If a bar refuses to add
    up after a careful re-reading, write it as you read it and flag it:

        % ragu: S4 M3 berjumlah 7/8, garis balok tidak terbaca jelas

7.5 Do not write chord diagrams or hand photographs into the notation. Chord
    letters printed above the number line are copied through as "D" or "A7"
    immediately before the note they sit over. Chord boxes at the bottom of the
    page are ignored.

7.6 Do not use these deprecated constructs: "+" as a chord delimiter, "+...+"
    decorations, the A: field, or a bare "Q:120".
